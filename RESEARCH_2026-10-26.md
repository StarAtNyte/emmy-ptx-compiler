# Kernel engineering review — 26 October 2026

This review covers GPU MODE, Modal, Unsloth, tinygrad, PufferAI and DeepSeek,
with selected recent compiler and verification papers. Repository revisions and
individual source hashes are recorded in
[research-refresh.json](validation/2026-10-26-research-refresh.json). Twenty source
files were inspected selectively across nine repositories; this is not a complete
code audit or a reproduction of their performance claims.

No source from these projects was incorporated into the compiler.

## Recent research

- [Taming Bitwise Behavior in GPU Kernels with Tensor Core](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11356),
  10 September 2026: reduction order, accumulation precision and rounding placement
  affect reproducibility. The full-text reduction/layout and checker sections were
  reviewed. The authors' equivalence and performance results were not reproduced.
  Our immediate response is exact cancellation and mixed-magnitude f32 tests;
  vector loading preserves the existing arithmetic sequence.
- [Racer: Hunting GPU Kernel Bugs with Symbolic Equivalence Checking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28429),
  20 October 2026: symbolic checking can expose logical bugs hidden by numerical
  tolerances. Abstract reviewed; Racer was not installed or run. A useful next
  direction is an independent checker for bounded address/alias transformations.
- [How to Train Your Little Dragon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20980),
  14 October 2026: optimization-script construction and refinement treat interacting
  transformations as a search problem. Abstract reviewed. Here, search must stay
  within the direct-PTX backend, fixed launch and numerical contract.
- [KernelOPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30059), revised 6 October 2026, and
  [HIERA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21157), 21 August 2026: verification and
  workload-aware implementation choices should constrain optimization. Abstracts
  reviewed. Library substitution and broader model rewrites are outside this
  compiler's submission contract; static, numerical, integration and performance
  checks remain separate acceptance conditions.

These are research directions, including preprints, rather than a claim that a
new method is proven faster or correct for this project.

## Work completed in this review

The four-wide shared-load experiment was already in progress when this review
was requested. It now has 84 structural cases, eight additional floating-point
stress cases, both-GPU checks for 59 changed retained kernels, and sanitizer
coverage of both boundary fixtures and three pipelined matmuls. Two existing CPU
snapshots were refreshed only after numerical checks; five new width/fallback
fixtures were retained. The local Python matrix has 130 checks per version.

Final seven-pair RTX 5090 results: public matmul 17.024→15.008 µs (1.134×),
independent 256×512×384 13.152→11.104 µs (1.184×). A third shape's larger discovery
advantage did not reproduce in the full sweep: 8.928→8.832 µs is retained as raw
measurement, not advertised as a robust speedup. The remaining 16 workloads differ
by at most 0.032 µs. Arithmetic order and f32 storage remain unchanged; no formal
whole-program equivalence proof is claimed.

## Next experiments

1. **Address proofs:** independently verify affine vector-load offsets, alignment
   and tail coverage, including signed 32-bit wraparound. Expand the accepted
   domain only when the proof and adversarial tests agree.
2. **Cache policy:** compare existing async-copy caching with a legal alternative
   on 16-byte copies from demonstrably read-only sources. Measure on the RTX 5090;
   keep current behavior if gains fail to repeat or other workloads regress.
3. **Bounded structural search:** evaluate a small width/expansion grid offline,
   deduplicating identical PTX and retaining compiler/device/IR/metadata hashes.
   Submission compilation remains deterministic and requires no GPU or tuning.
4. **Profile the remaining attention cost:** collect target traces/counters when
   the execution environment supports them. Use a capability check; neither a
   utilization percentage nor local sm86 data establishes an sm120 bottleneck.

The repository remains private, and contest submission remains deferred.
